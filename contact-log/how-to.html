The Teacher's Desk Contact Log How to

Contact Log

A record of every conversation you have with a parent or guardian — the phone call at 4pm, the email you fired off between classes, the doorway chat at pickup. One message slip per contact, and about ten seconds each.

Adding a slip

Open the log, pick a class, then pick a student. The blank slip is already at the top with today's date filled in. Tick how you made contact, tick what happened, type a line or two, and add it to the record. Ctrl+Enter saves without reaching for the mouse.

The result row matters more than it looks. “Left message” and “Reached them” are very different things when someone asks whether you made contact, and a month later you will not remember which it was.

Who you haven't reached

The class screen is a card file sorted coldest first — students you've never contacted sit at the top with a red tab, then whoever you spoke to longest ago. That ordering is the whole point. Filter to Never contacted before a calling night, or Follow-ups owed to see who you promised to get back to.

Printing a record

When an administrator, a parent, or an IEP meeting asks what contact you've made, use Print record on a student — you get a plain dated table of every contact. Print class log does the same for everyone, and CSV hands you the raw rows for a spreadsheet.

Hiding it in a hurry

Students walk past your screen all day. Press Esc — even mid-sentence — and the log drops behind a blank card that says nothing about anyone. Press Esc again to bring it back. The hide button in the top-right does the same thing for a mouse.

If a name changes

Fix a spelling or change a student's name in the Seating Chart and the log follows them automatically. If it can't tell who's who — two names changing at once, say — the record waits under Not on the current roster so you can point it at the right student. Nothing you've written is ever deleted because a roster changed.

Where this lives

In this browser, on this device. No account, no server, nothing sent anywhere — which also means it isn't backed up for you. Export a copy from Settings now and then, especially before a school laptop refresh.
